Implications from the letter (interpretive, logged as hypotheses):
- K5 is an **alternate ending / alternate K4** written in 1988 at the same time as K4 and sized for the same slot (97). Its plaintext may share much more than BERLINCLOCK (H-1303).

## 3. Analysis of K5 against K4: not possible yet; prepared method
No K5 text exists to align. What the statement implies, and what to do on release:
1. **The 64-74 test (decisive).** Both plaintexts carry BERLINCLOCK at 64-74. If K5[64..74] = NYPVTTMZFPK, the keystream at 64-74 is identical in the two messages even though the plaintexts differ elsewhere. The key at those positions is then a function of position, plus at most a shared plaintext prefix. That refutes autokey or ciphertext-feedback unless the K4 and K5 plaintexts are also identical before 64. If the ciphertext at 64-74 differs, the "similar" coding uses a different key (or alphabet). The two messages are then not in depth, but the 11 known plaintext letters of K5 become a fresh crib set, and comparing them with K4's tests whether the key has the same structure.
2. **Depth attack if the keys coincide.** Wherever K4 and K5 share a key, C5 - C4 = P5 - P4 (for Vigenere-type, in whichever alphabet is used). Runs of identical ciphertext at the same position mark runs of identical plaintext, which is likely for an "alternate ending". Elsewhere, two-text depth reconstruction is classical (Kerckhoffs superimposition / running-key style, solvable with English n-gram models). With 24 K4 crib letters and 11 K5 crib letters, it is strongly constrained.
3. **If the keys differ by a constant or periodic offset** ("similar but not identical"), C5 - C4 at the 64-74 crib positions gives the key difference directly, and a constant run would show up at once.
4. Script: `python3 workers/w-k5/k5_depth.py <K5>` prints the identical-ciphertext runs (random expectation is about 3.7 matches out of 97) and the K4 and K5 key letters at 64-74 under Vig, Beau and vBeau with AZ and KA alphabets. It also prints C5-C4 for both alphabets. Self-test: the K4 KA-Vig key at 64-74 is ELYOIECBAQK, which matches w-history.

## 4. What the public auction photos show (K4 "original coding system": NOT shown)
I examined all 14 RR lot images (https://cdn.rrauction.com/auction/730/medium/3507616_{291,2,4,9,10,11,19,21,22,23,24,27,30,31}.jpg), the two full-resolution RR blog images, and the Paradigm page images. **None shows the K4 coding system, a K4 or K5 coding chart, K5 text or the K4 plaintext**, which is consistent with RR withholding items 1-7. What is visible:
- **Copper proof-of-concept plate** (_2, _4, _9, blog img 1): three rows of cut serif letters, CDEFGHIJ / DEFGHIJK / EFGHIJKL. These are consecutive plain A-Z alphabet runs, each row shifted by one, like the upper-left corner of a plain tableau. The edge is stamped "J.S.1988". This is a cutting test (per Sanborn's letter), not a cipher chart.
- **Two handwritten yellow legal-pad pages** (_21, also visible in _27). These are the K1 and K2 plaintext and two drafts of the K3 plaintext. Visible details:
  - K1 is spelled correctly as "ILLUSION", so the IQLUSION error entered at the coding stage, which agrees with the PALIMPCEST key error.
  - K2 ends "...seconds west X Layer two." with "X" separators throughout.
  - Hundreds markers ("100", "200", "300") are written above the text, so letter counts were tracked.
  - K3 draft page header: boxed "11 Lines | 342" and boxed "3lines 93", with a faint "336 3 Lines(?)" above. The second K3 page has boxed "10.84 rows" (one digit unclear).
  - Reading (mine): 336/31 = 10.84, 11 x 31 = 341, and 3 x 31 = 93. So Sanborn laid out the bottom panel in rows of 31: K3 (336) + "?" + OBKR fills 11 rows, and K4's remaining 93 letters fill 3 rows. This matches the sculpture layout already in facts. "342" vs 341 is an unclear reading or count.
  - Small per-line numbers (51, 53, 57, 77) appear in the margins.
  - Nothing K4 is visible on these sheets.
- **Background of the hero image** (_291 / blog img 2, 2100x3033): two sheets covered in a regular grid of typed or printed characters, one apparently ruled into cells. They are deliberately out of focus and **illegible** at full resolution. I cannot say what they are; they may be charts.
- **Paradigm "Mini Kryptos Archive Item"** photo (images.ctfassets.net ... Mini_Kryptos_Archive_Item_Paradigm__2_.jpg): a miniature etched brass or steel replica of the sculpture text. Its K4 rows read the same as the carved text ("?OBKR / UOXOGHULBSOLIFBBWFLRVQQPRNGKSSO / ... / ...DIGKUHUAUEKCAR"), and the KA tableau is on the right half. In the foreground is a **hand-lettered tableau on grid paper**: rows of the KRYPTOS alphabet (K R Y P T O S A B C D E F G H ...), each row shifted by one (diagonals read T O S A B C D E F G H). This is a KA Vigenere tableau, the K1/K2 type. Only about 8x10 cells are visible. Whether it is an original Sanborn chart or a Paradigm prop is not stated.
- **Other items:** the escort badge "No 13764" (dated "15 MAY 1991"?); the GSA contract (#GS-11P88EGC0237, $250,000, 29 Jul 1988); a modification for $5,000; the dedication program and pamphlet (5 Nov 1990); and photographs.
- **Kobek/Byrne (Zona Motel, 17 Oct 2025):** the Smithsonian files held "scraps of cut-up paper"; there is no mention of charts or tableau. RR says those were 1990 scrambled copies, not encryption materials. Byrne (AFP): "I took images of all the coding stuff in the files". Those images are unpublished.
